# Why it misses
Unions say 'it's one of these' (narrowing), intersections say 'it's all of these simultaneously' (merging) — they have opposite semantics.

<Steps>
  <Step icon="flag" title="Union: OR">
    <code>A | B</code> means the value is <kw>either A or B</kw>. You must narrow before accessing properties unique to one side.
  </Step>
  <Step icon="box" title="Intersection: AND">
    <code>A & B</code> means the value is <kw>both A and B</kw>. It must satisfy all constraints simultaneously — the result has all properties from both.
  </Step>
  <Step icon="gear" title="When each breaks">
    Unions break when you forget to narrow — accessing a property not on the common shape throws. Intersections break when the types contradict — <code>string & number</code> becomes <code>never</code>.
  </Step>
  <Step icon="check" title="Practical pattern">
    Use unions for variants (success/error), intersections for mixins (adding fields to an existing type).
  </Step>
</Steps>
